# Lens Selection Rules

## Decision test

Score each candidate lens from 0–2 on six criteria:

1. **Decision relevance:** would the split change a real marketing decision?
2. **Explanatory separation:** do the poles differ in desired action, barrier, evidence need or journey?
3. **Observability:** could later research recognize the difference through behavior or language?
4. **Actionability:** can the team adapt message, proof, experience, channel role or offer?
5. **Non-redundancy:** does it add something not already captured by another axis?
6. **Evidence plausibility:** is there a brief-grounded reason to investigate it without claiming it is true?

Choose the strongest lens, not necessarily the highest numerical total. Use the score to expose judgment, not simulate certainty. A lens scoring 0 on decision relevance or observability cannot be primary.

## Common business patterns

| Business problem | Start by testing | Useful secondary lens |
|---|---|---|
| Skincare or symptom-led care | pain/need type, severity, trigger, current coping or failed solution | evidence threshold, regimen maturity, sensitivity/risk |
| New creative or AI tool | mindset/readiness or adoption stage | expertise, workflow integration, proof threshold |
| Mature FMCG or food | occasion, job and household context | repertoire, routine, value trade-off |
| Fashion, wellness or status category | identity/cultural orientation | occasion, insider fluency, public visibility |
| B2B or multi-person purchase | decision role and workflow/job | organizational maturity, risk/proof threshold |
| Subscription/retention | relationship stage and habit | unmet progress, switching trigger |
| Premiumization | value sought and category involvement | identity signal, willingness-to-pay evidence |
| Audience expansion | anti-funnel proximity | education, permission, proof or accessibility barrier |

These are starting hypotheses. The brief may justify a different choice.

## State versus scene

- Choose **state** when people in the same scene need different reassurance, proof or activation paths.
- Choose **scene** when timing, setting, companions, constraints or stakes materially change the need and behavior.
- Pair them only when both independently change the decision.
- Never target a scene merely because it is vivid; never invent a state merely because it sounds insightful.

## Distinguish four kinds of audience variables

| Kind | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic lens | What difference explains a different response? | try-first vs proof-starved |
| Qualification filter | Who must be in scope? | lives in launch market; has recurring acne; controls budget |
| Access label | Where can later research find them? | gaming, 3D printing, developer or AI communities |
| Descriptor | What helps describe or represent them? | age, city tier, household, profession |

Promote a filter, label or descriptor to a strategic lens only when evidence suggests it changes the marketing response.

## Failure modes

- **Demographic default:** treating age or gender as explanation rather than context.
- **Interest taxonomy:** confusing findability with motivation.
- **Persona theatre:** inventing names, biographies, quotes or polished archetypes before evidence.
- **Matrix explosion:** crossing many dimensions into unusable microsegments.
- **Fixed-type fallacy:** treating a temporary state, adoption stage or behavior as identity.
- **Scene glamour:** selecting a visually attractive scene with no different job or constraint.
- **Innovation theatre:** calling every enthusiastic buyer an innovator or assuming the classic diffusion proportions.
- **Premature targeting:** selecting a commercial target before validating existence, size, value and reachability.
- **Platform absolutism:** treating a platform's proprietary labels or dated audience counts as universal truth.

## Minimum recommendation rule

Return one primary lens, at most one secondary lens, necessary filters/descriptors, and 2–4 provisional contrasts. If the brief cannot support even a provisional choice, recommend the two best competing lenses and name the single question that would decide between them.
