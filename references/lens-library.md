# Audience Lens Library

Use this as a menu, not a checklist. A lens earns primary status only when it would change a marketing decision.

| Lens | Best used when | Possible contrasts to validate | Avoid when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Need / pain / job | The category is entered to solve a concrete problem; common in skincare, health, repair and services | pain type, severity, trigger, desired progress, failed solution history | the same pain produces no meaningful difference in response |
| Mindset / decision state | People with similar interests or circumstances differ mainly in readiness, confidence or evidence needs | try-first vs proof-starved; curious vs threatened; control-seeking vs relief-seeking | states cannot be observed or tied to a decision |
| Behavior / occasion / context | Need, constraints or product role change by when, where, with whom or why the behavior happens | planned vs urgent; private vs social; routine vs exceptional; at-home vs mobile | “scene” is only a creative backdrop and does not alter behavior |
| Category expertise / involvement | Novices, enthusiasts and professionals evaluate quality differently | casual, committed, expert; DIY, prosumer, professional; light vs intensive user | expertise is being inferred from job title or self-image alone |
| Adoption stage / diffusion role | The offer or behavior is genuinely new and the challenge is moving from experimentation to broader adoption | innovators, early adopters, early majority, late majority, laggards—operationalized by risk, proof, compatibility and peer influence | the category is mature or labels merely flatter users as innovators |
| Solution relationship / switching stage | The growth problem is entry, switching, upgrading, lapsing or return | non-user, workaround user, competitor user, trialist, adopter, loyalist, lapsed | stages are treated as fixed identities rather than a journey |
| Lifecycle / transition | Life or role transition changes needs and constraints | first job, new parent, new home, caregiving, retirement; category-specific transitions | age is used as a proxy without the actual transition |
| Decision role / buying unit | Buyer, user, influencer, gatekeeper or beneficiary differ; common in B2B, family and healthcare | economic buyer vs user; parent vs child; recommender vs approver | a single-person purchase has no meaningful role split |
| Value / identity / cultural orientation | Choice expresses belonging, distinction, taste, morality or self-concept | private utility vs public signal; insider fluency vs aspirational learning | identity claims are speculative or insensitive and cannot be validated |
| Anti-funnel proximity | The task is to grow from a core to adjacent or broad audience | core expert, interest-adjacent, broad need-state; education and permission barriers between rings | it becomes a reach-volume funnel with no cultural or learning logic |
| Value / economics | Price, risk and expected return materially reshape the decision | value-seeking, performance-seeking, prestige-seeking; low vs high switching cost | income is used as a crude proxy for willingness to pay |
| Relationship / loyalty | Retention, advocacy or community participation is the decision | transactional, habitual, attached, advocate; trust and reciprocity | the objective is first-time category creation only |

## Combining lenses

Use at most two strategic axes by default:

- **Primary mindset + secondary expertise:** useful when novices and professionals can each be optimistic or proof-starved.
- **Primary pain + secondary severity or solution history:** useful for skincare and symptom-led categories.
- **Primary occasion + secondary household role:** useful for food, home and family categories.
- **Primary adoption stage + secondary use case:** useful for new creative tools and emerging behaviors.
- **Primary anti-funnel ring + secondary proof barrier:** useful when a specialist practice must become accessible without losing authority.

If a two-axis matrix creates cells that would receive the same marketing response, collapse it.

## Adoption-stage operationalization

Do not assign diffusion categories from enthusiasm alone. Examine:

- willingness to tolerate bugs, uncertainty or social risk;
- need for peer, expert, institutional or outcome proof;
- compatibility with current routines and tools;
- reversibility, learning cost and switching cost;
- ability and motivation to influence others;
- whether adoption is experimental, occasional or integrated into workflow.

The canonical five stages are a hypothesis framework, not observed segment sizes or permanent personality types.
