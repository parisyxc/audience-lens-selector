# Consumer Persona Evaluation

Choose **how to look at people before researching them**. Produce a compact audience-lens brief that guides later social listening or user research; do not pretend the research has already happened.

## Principles

- Start from the marketing decision, not a favorite segmentation model.
- A useful split changes the message, proof, product experience, channel role, or adoption path.
- Treat demographics as boundaries or descriptors unless they explain a material difference in response.
- Distinguish `strategic lens`, `qualification filter`, `access label`, and `profile descriptor`.
- A scene is an observable context; a state is a person's current relationship to the decision. Choose whichever better explains the response, or pair one primary axis with one secondary axis.
- Treat segment names, motivations, sizes, and prevalence as hypotheses until researched.
- Keep facts, client claims, interpretations, hypotheses, and unknowns separate.

## Inputs

Read the full brief and any supplied context. Extract:

1. Business type, category, offer, price and route to market.
2. Marketing objective and the decision this audience work must inform.
3. Desired human action or behavior change.
4. Existing audience claims and their evidence status.
5. Constraints: geography, eligibility, current users, timing, budget and channels.
6. The most consequential unknown about why people would act, hesitate, switch, adopt or advocate.

If information is missing, proceed with explicit assumptions and ask only questions that could change the recommended lens.

## Workflow

### 1. Frame the audience decision

Write one sentence:

> We need to understand differences in ___ so the team can decide ___ without assuming ___.

### 2. Generate candidate lenses

Consult [lens-library.md](references/lens-library.md). Generate only lenses supported by the business problem. Include category-specific classics when appropriate—for example, pain/need states in skincare or adoption stage in an emerging creative technology.

### 3. Separate the layers

Classify each proposed variable:

- **Primary strategic lens:** best explains meaningfully different marketing responses.
- **Secondary lens:** adds necessary context without creating a sprawling matrix.
- **Qualification filters:** define who is eligible or commercially relevant.
- **Access labels:** interests, communities, search behaviors or professions that help later researchers find people.
- **Profile descriptors:** demographics and other facts used to describe, size or check representation.

Do not confuse an easy-to-find media label with the strategic segmentation. “Gamers,” “3D-printing users,” and “AI enthusiasts” may be access labels while “try-first optimists” and “proof-starved professionals” are provisional decision states.

### 4. Score and select

Use [selection-rules.md](references/selection-rules.md). Select:

- one primary lens;
- zero or one secondary lens;
- only the minimum necessary filters and descriptors.

Prefer the lens that creates the clearest contrast in desired action, barrier, evidence need, message or journey. Explicitly reject one or two tempting but weaker cuts.

### 5. Operationalize for later research

Turn the selected lens into observable contrasts. For each provisional group or pole specify:

- working definition;
- inclusion and exclusion signals;
- behavior, language or proof sought later;
- the marketing decision that would change if the group exists;
- what evidence would falsify the hypothesis.

Never invent quotes, prevalence, segment size or psychological certainty.

### 6. Produce the handoff

Follow [output-contract.md](references/output-contract.md). Default to a concise answer in the conversation. Save a file only when requested.

## Boundary

This skill stops after recommending the research angle and validation design. It does **not**:

- scrape platforms, search the web, read social conversations or run social listening;
- claim an insight or human truth has been validated;
- write a fictional named persona, empathy map or finished audience portrait;
- calculate segment size or select a final commercial target;
- prescribe media targeting merely because a platform label exists.

Hand the output to the relevant social-listening, digital-ethnography, interview, survey or analytics workflow.
